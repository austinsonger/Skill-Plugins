Defensible evidence is documented proof that is irrefutable and legally survivable — capable of withstanding scrutiny from auditors, regulators, insurers, class action litigants, or prosecutors. It should be the natural byproduct of operating a secure, compliant, and resilient program, not manufactured for audits.

The CISO's defensible position must prove: *"Leadership was informed of the risk, the controls were recommended, and leadership knowingly accepted the risk."*

**Evidence Category Assessment:**

For each category, ask the user to rate their current state (Strong / Partial / Missing) and note specific gaps:

**A3.1 — Governance Evidence** (demonstrates executive leadership oversight)
- Cybersecurity program charter
- Cybersecurity strategy (multi-year roadmap with budget requests)
- Quarterly Business Review (QBR) briefing materials
- Security steering committee meeting minutes
- Published, version-controlled policies and standards

**A3.2 — Risk Evidence** (demonstrates risks were identified and managed)
- Risk Management Program (RMP) documentation
- Supply Chain Risk Management (SCRM) plan
- Formal risk assessments (including DPIAs, SCRAs)
- Threat catalog (entity-specific)
- Risk catalog and risk register / POA&M
- Control gap analysis
- Signed risk acceptance documentation

**A3.3 — Security Capability Evidence** (demonstrates controls exist and operate)
- Documented policies, standards, and procedures
- System Security Plan (SSP) or similar
- Secure Baseline Configurations (SBC) documentation
- IT Asset Management (ITAM) program evidence
- IAM program documentation
- Incident Response Plans (IRPs)
- Security event monitoring logs and reports
- Vulnerability scan results and VPMP documentation

**A3.4 — Compliance Evidence** (demonstrates regulatory/contractual conformity)
- Inventory of applicable laws, regulations, contracts
- Framework conformity evidence (audit results, assessment reports)
- Evidence of non-conformity governance (compensating controls, POA&M)

**A3.5 — Resilience Evidence** (demonstrates recovery capability)
- Incident response reports (post-incident)
- Root Cause Analysis (RCA) documentation
- IRP tabletop/functional exercise reports
- Disaster recovery test results
- Business continuity exercise reports

**A3.6 — Assurance Evidence** (demonstrates independent validation)
- Third-party audit reports
- Penetration testing reports
- Regulatory inspection reports

**Annex 4 — CISO Defensibility Checklist (A4.1–A4.9):**

Assess each of the 9 CISO defensibility actions (In Place / Partial / Missing):

- A4.1: Formal cybersecurity strategy & implementation plan (3–5 year roadmap, budget requests, status reports)
- A4.2: Documented risk assessments and BIAs (DPIAs, SCRAs, threat modeling, BIA)
- A4.3: Formal risk register (with likelihood, impact, affected assets, remediation cost, risk owner, acceptance status)
- A4.4: Incidents tracking (prevented attacks, occurred incidents, phishing results, pentest findings)
- A4.5: Third-party assessments (independent audits, pentests, maturity assessments)
- A4.6: CISO Journal / documented decisions (chronological log of analysis, decisions, and stakeholder interactions including rejected budget requests)
- A4.7: Risk reporting to executive leadership (QBR briefings, board/audit/risk committee presentations)
- A4.8: Formal risk acceptance from leadership (RAF, board minutes, or email summaries documenting leadership's knowing acceptance of risk)
- A4.9: Evidence of professional competence (scoped policies, SBCs, tested IRP, security training, proactive vuln management)

**Output: Defensible Evidence Plan**

Produce a structured document containing:

1. **Evidence Program Summary** — current posture, primary gaps, key risk to CISO/organization
2. **Evidence Inventory Table** — Category | Items Present | Items Missing | Priority to Address
3. **CISO Defensibility Scorecard** — A4.1–A4.9 with status and specific gaps noted
4. **Top 10 Evidence Actions** — prioritized list of specific evidence items to create or strengthen, with rationale
5. **Evidence Retention Guidance** — what to retain, how long, where (including eDiscovery-accessible formats)
6. **Escalation Path Reminder** — the recommended CISO escalation path when leadership rejects security funding: CIO → Executive Leadership → Board Risk Committee → Board Audit Committee
