Conduct a structured SCRMS gap assessment for an entity using the Security, Compliance & Resilience Management System (SCRMS) framework from the SCF Council.

Work through the following areas interactively, asking the user questions one section at a time. Wait for their responses before moving to the next section.

**Step 1 — Establish Context (Principle 1)**
Ask:
- What industry/sector is the entity in?
- What is the entity's approximate size (employees, revenue range)?
- What countries/regions does it operate in?
- Who is the primary audience for this assessment (CISO, GRC team, board, auditor)?

**Step 2 — Compliance Obligations (MCR)**
Ask about applicable laws, regulations, and contracts:
- Statutory: HIPAA, FISMA, FERPA, GLBA, CCPA/CPRA, SOX, FACTA, etc.
- Regulatory: DFARS/CMMC, FedRAMP, NY DFS 23 NYCRR 500, EU GDPR, etc.
- Contractual: PCI DSS, ISO 27001, SOC 2, NIST CSF, CIS CSC, etc.

**Step 3 — Risk Appetite & DSR**
Ask:
- Has the entity defined a risk appetite statement?
- Are there any internally-driven security requirements beyond compliance (e.g., board directives, internal audit findings, prior incidents)?
- Has the entity defined materiality thresholds for incidents, risks, and threats?

**Step 4 — SCRMS Principles Assessment**
For each of the 9 SCRMS Principles, ask whether it has been addressed (Yes / Partial / No):
1. Establish Context — documented mission, business context, compliance obligations?
2. Identify Applicable Controls — is there a Living Control Set (LCS) or equivalent?
3. Define Maturity Expectations — are control maturity targets defined?
4. Publish Governance Documentation — are policies, standards, procedures published and enforced?
5. Assign Stakeholder Accountability — are control owners assigned across business units?
6. Prioritize Capabilities According to Risk — is there a risk-based remediation roadmap?
7. Maintain Situational Awareness — are metrics, monitoring, and audits in place?
8. Manage Risk — is there a formal risk register with treatment decisions?
9. Evolve Processes — is there a PDCA improvement cycle with documented updates?

**Step 5 — Domain Coverage Spot-Check**
Ask the user to rate (Green/Yellow/Red) key domains:
- Governance & Compliance (Sections 11.1, 11.8)
- Identity & Access Management (Section 11.16)
- Vulnerability & Patch Management (Section 11.32)
- Incident Response (Section 11.17)
- Risk Management (Section 11.25)
- Third-Party Management (Section 11.30)
- Data Classification & Privacy (Sections 11.12, 11.23)
- Business Continuity & DR (Section 11.4)

**Step 6 — Defensible Evidence Check**
Ask whether the following evidence categories exist (Yes / Partial / No):
- Governance evidence (program charter, steering committee minutes, policies)
- Risk evidence (risk register, DPIAs, threat catalog)
- Security capability evidence (SSP, asset inventory, IRP)
- Compliance evidence (audit results, POA&M)
- Resilience evidence (BC/DR test results, incident response exercises)
- Assurance evidence (third-party audits, penetration tests)

---

After collecting all responses, produce a **SCRMS Gap Assessment Report** containing:

1. **Executive Summary** — overall posture (Secure & Compliant / Partially Compliant / At Risk / Negligent quadrant per SCRMS)
2. **Compliance Obligations Summary** — MCR identified, gaps noted
3. **Principles Gap Table** — 9 principles with status and gap description
4. **Domain Gap Summary** — color-coded status for each spot-checked domain
5. **Evidence Gaps** — which evidence categories are missing or incomplete
6. **Prioritized Remediation Roadmap** — top 10 actions ranked by risk, mapped to SCRMS principle and SCF control domain
7. **Quick Wins** — 3–5 low-effort, high-impact actions to take immediately

Format the report clearly using headers and tables where appropriate.
